I am a CNRS researcher (CRCN) at MSC, Université Paris Cité, working at the crossroads of soft matter, hydrodynamics, phase change, and viscoelasticity — with a recurring taste for multiphase problems. My approach is experimental, and I try to keep what I see in the lab in close conversation with theoretical models.

I completed my PhD at LadHyX (École polytechnique) and PMMH (ESPCI) under the supervision of Christophe Clanet and David Quéré, working on the spontaneous dynamics of Leidenfrost drops. I then spent three years as a postdoc in the Physics of Fluids group at the University of Twente with Jacco Snoeijer, where I extended my interest to viscoelastic fluids, and condensation on soft substrates. I joined MSC as a CNRS researcher in 2023.

My ongoing research revolves around phase transitions, interfacial hydrodynamics, and soft matter, through several intertwined projects: condensation of water vapor on soft polymer gels (with Bruno Andreotti, LPENS, Uwe Thiele, Münster, and Jacco Snoeijer, UTwente); homogeneous nucleation and condensation in synthetic polymer blends, inspired by living cells (with Mathilde Reyssat and Élie Raphaël, Gulliver — ANR JCJC MORNING); evaporation of volatile baths enhanced by surface bubbles, through thermocapillary Marangoni flows (with Axel Huerre and Matthieu Roché, MSC); drying and nucleation in salted hydrogels, used as model soils to study the polygonal patterns of salt flats (IdEx Emergence SECOS); the temperature of soap films, shaped by evaporation (with Axel Huerre and Adrien Bussonnière, MSC); the thermocapillary destabilization of viscous bubbles, and the rebound of liquid drops on hot moving surfaces (both with Martin Coux, SGR).

Beyond research, I am involved in equality and inclusion initiatives within my community, and I enjoy bringing physics to schools and to the general public.
